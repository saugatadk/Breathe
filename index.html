Where this comes from

Every claim on this page traces to one of these. Where a study contradicts the popular version of a technique, that's noted on the technique itself rather than buried here.

  • Balban et al. 2023, Cell Reports Medicine — five minutes a day of cyclic sighing, box breathing, cyclic hyperventilation or mindfulness meditation, compared over a month in 108 adults.
  • Fincham et al. 2023, Scientific Reports — meta-analysis of breathwork trials for stress and mental health.
  • Fincham et al. 2023, Scientific Reports — coherent breathing tested against a sham breathwork placebo.
  • Lehrer & Gevirtz 2014, Frontiers in Psychology — why breathing near 0.1 Hz produces such large heart-rate oscillations.
  • Manzoni et al. 2008, BMC Psychiatry — ten years of relaxation training trials for anxiety, reviewed.
  • Progressive muscle relaxation in adults — review of effects on stress, anxiety and depression.
  • Albulescu et al. 2022, PLOS ONE — micro-breaks of ten minutes or less across 22 studies.
  • Lee et al. 2015, Journal of Environmental Psychology — a forty-second look at a green roof, and what it did to sustained attention.
  • Oppezzo & Schwartz 2014, JEP: Learning, Memory, and Cognition — walking and divergent thinking.
  • Ramirez & Beilock 2011, Science — ten minutes of writing about exam worries, and the exam scores that followed.
  • Talens-Estarelles et al. 2022 and a 2025 break-schedule trial — what screen breaks actually do to eye strain.
  • Acute mindfulness induction in high-anxiety adults and Palmer et al. 2023 — how much a single short sit is worth.
  • Kjaer et al. 2002, Cognitive Brain Research — the eight-person PET study behind every NSDR dopamine claim you've read.

This is a study aid, not medical care. Paced breathing can make you lightheaded; stop and breathe normally if it does. If stress or anxiety is affecting your daily life, talk to a doctor or your university's counselling service — a website is not a substitute for that.
